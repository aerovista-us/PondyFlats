New automation tools available for Design 4 refinement

The current LotScope automation layer does not force one repair strategy. It can compare building translation/re-proportioning, garage movement/resize, driveway reshaping, and localized pavement/apron expansion as peer options while retaining the hard parcel, setback, vehicle, turn, collision, door, and parking gates.

More pavement remains an option

Pavement sensitivity can test whether a wider local corridor/apron actually clears the remaining hard failure before spending more site area.

Buildings can move

Residential mass can shift or re-proportion inside the legal/program envelope when that creates a cleaner vehicle corridor.

Garages can move or resize

Detached garage position and credible plate size can trade against access geometry instead of being treated as fixed.

Garage rotation is experimental

The platform can now represent oriented detached-garage polygons, doors, and parked poses, but rotation is not yet part of the proven Design 4 evidence shown here.

The general LotScope platform benchmark currently retains 40 physical passes, 40 combined passes, and five distinct promotion-ready/frozen concepts. Those general finalists are separate from this Design 4 four-stall package; the numbers are platform context, not a new Design 4 run.

What remains conditional

The geometry gate passes, but the daily-use experience is not yet perfect: the Garage B north path is a multi-point maneuver using four gear changes, and the Garage B south path has a 0.073 ft (about 0.9 in) modeled door margin. The new automation layer can compare pavement expansion against building/garage movement and driveway reshaping rather than assuming one fix. Workbench labels these as refinement targets instead of silently promoting them. Parcel-specific accessory zoning still requires confirmation, and the approximately 2 ft clear gap between the two rear garages remains a building/fire/eave/drainage/AHJ review item.

Professional-validation boundary

This package supports concept selection and design development. Zoning, building/fire code, structural, MEP, civil, drainage, survey/easement, energy, and permit validation remain professional/AHJ work. Nothing here is labeled permit-ready, code-approved, zoning-approved, structurally validated, civil-certified, or construction-ready.
