Lot 2 duplex study

The project has evolved from a simple “can two homes fit?” exercise into a disciplined site-planning study focused on finding the best two-home configuration for an unusual 7,028 SF parcel on Pennsylvania Avenue.
7,028 SF · 0.161 AC50.00′ Pennsylvania frontage148.00′ long axis57.01′ west/rear boundary2 × ~1,800 SF homes2 × 22′×22′ garages
Current direction

Stop adding shapes. Start proving them.

Concept discovery is essentially complete. The next phase is an apples-to-apples mathematical comparison of seven genuinely different site strategies, followed by finished architectural elevations only after each math pass is approved.

Key discipline: math first, finished render second. The pretty image must inherit the measured geometry rather than reinvent it.

2. Program evolution

Original benchmark

900 SF first floor + 900 SF second floor

This remains the straightforward test program for rectangular concepts such as E1, E2, E3, F1, G1 and G2.

Important V2 lesson

750 SF lower + ~1,050 SF upper

The mathematically constructed V showed that the total ~1,800 SF requirement matters more than a rigid 900/900 split. Moving area upstairs can substantially improve site efficiency.

4. What we learned from the earlier concepts

Single-home work

A · B · C remain useful

Side-drive/rear-garage, L-shaped, and front-courtyard concepts remain valid as a separate single-home lane on the concept site.

False variety problem

D2 / E3 / E4 converged too much

Later math diagrams flattened them into nearly identical east-west rows. That triggered the shift to seven genuinely different strategies.

Image-generation lesson

Labels are not dimensions

A rendered “22×22 garage” is not proof that the object is actually drawn at that scale. Mathematical construction now precedes finished rendering.

Orientation lesson

Pennsylvania is always east/right

The V and other concepts only make sense when the street frontage is kept fixed and the long axis runs westward into the site.

5. V2 mathematical breakthrough

6. Two-pass rendering workflow

1

Mathematical pass

Use the exact survey polygon, exact house footprints, garage boxes, driveway widths, clearances, and calculated areas. No decorative interpretation yet.

2

Approve geometry

Confirm orientation, square footage, garage placement, circulation, court/yard size, and whether the concept still deserves advancement.

3

Finished pass

Create the landscaped site plan, Pennsylvania-facing elevation, 3/4 perspective, materials, roof form, porches, garage treatment, and private-yard visualization.

8. Current direction

The project should now move from broad ideation into controlled comparative testing.

1

E2 Recessed Garage first

Use E2 as the conventional benchmark. If V2 cannot materially outperform it on value, privacy, or character, the premium complexity may not be justified.

2

Then E1 → E3 → F1 → G1 → G2

Rebuild each concept on the exact same coordinate system and program basis.

3

Track comparable metrics

First-floor conditioned area, upper-floor target, total living area, garage area, paved area, yard/court area, minimum separation, driveway length, exterior corners, roof complexity, and likely cost premium.

4

Update the Concept Options site

Once the seven math passes exist, the website should become a real decision tool instead of a gallery of attractive diagrams.

10. Core design rule going forward

SURVEY GEOMETRY → MATHEMATICAL FOOTPRINT → GARAGE + DRIVE TEST → APPROVE FIT → FLOOR-AREA TEST → FINISHED SITE PLAN → PENNSYLVANIA ELEVATION → 3/4 PERSPECTIVE → WEBSITE UPDATE
Concept planning only. Survey geometry is the common base. Final setbacks, fire separation, utilities, easements, drainage, vehicle maneuvering, structural design, and ownership configuration require professional / agency verification before design development.
