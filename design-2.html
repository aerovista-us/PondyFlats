How to read these drawings

Four rules cover every sheet. If something looks “sideways,” it is because the lot is drawn as it sits on Pennsylvania — not rotated to make north point up.

1 · Front of the lot

Pennsylvania is on the right

That is the street, the 50′ frontage, and where cars enter. Home A is the east home, closer to the street. Home B sits farther back, forming an L.

2 · Rear of the lot

Garages are on the left

The two green 20×20 boxes are detached two-car garages in the rear yard. Garage A is the north (driveway-side) garage. Garage B is the south garage.

3 · Dashed lines

Gold is for homes; green is for garages

The gold dashed line is the principal building envelope (20′ front, 25′ rear, 5′ / 10′ sides). The green dashed band is where a detached garage may sit under the lots-without-alley reading.

4 · Honesty

Outlines are real; finishes are not

Building sizes, ridges, and garage doors come from the lock. Stone, windows, and room names are conceptual. Color on the plans is room type, not finish. A prettier picture that moves a wall is the wrong picture.

Floor plans

Read the diagram first, then the dimensioned plans. Circles are rooms; a line means those rooms share a wall. Color is room type (living, kitchen, bedroom…). The boxes on A-101 / A-102 are the same rooms, fitted to plates that already work for parking. They are not a permit plan or a square-footage takeoff.
