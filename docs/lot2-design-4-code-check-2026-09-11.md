# Pondy Flats Lot 2 — Design 4 accessory-code check

**Checked:** 2026-09-11  
**Purpose:** Verify the Design 4 planning assumptions against the currently published Coeur d'Alene Municipal Code before promoting a discovery geometry.  
**Status:** Current-code evidence for design development; **not a zoning determination, permit approval, or substitute for City review**.

## Current published code

American Legal Publishing's Coeur d'Alene code site states the code is current through **Ordinance 3779, passed July 7, 2026**.

### §17.06.425 — accessory structures in the rear 25 ft

For accessory structures located in the rear twenty-five feet of a lot, the published code requires:

- **5 ft minimum side setback** as the normal rule.
- **5 ft minimum rear setback** as the normal rule.
- A 3 ft side setback is an exception only when the roof does not slope toward that side property line.
- A 3 ft rear setback is an exception only under the stated roof/alley conditions.
- A detached accessory structure may encroach up to 3 ft beyond the 25 ft rear-yard line while maintaining the stated setbacks when the detached structure is no more than 18 ft high.

Design 4 intentionally uses the normal **5 ft / 5 ft** accessory targets and does not rely on the 3 ft exceptions.

Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/coeurdaleneid/latest/coeurdalene_id/0-0-0-11058

### §17.06.630 — accessory-structure criteria

The published code says a structure can qualify as accessory rather than being subject to the principal structure's site-performance standards when, among other criteria, it is detached and separated from the principal structure by **6 ft or more**. A structure with sleeping/living accommodations is not treated under this ordinary accessory-structure rule.

Design 4 therefore keeps **6 ft minimum garage-to-principal-building separation as a planning floor** and forbids living/sleeping space in the garages.

Source: https://codelibrary.amlegal.com/codes/coeurdaleneid/latest/coeurdalene_id/0-0-0-11124

### §17.05.190 / §17.05.210 — R-12 accessory garages and rear-yard height

The published R-12 schedule lists an attached or detached garage/carport as an accessory permitted use. For detached accessory buildings in the rear yard, the published height table shows **14 ft for low/no-slope roofs** and **18 ft for medium/high-slope roofs**.

## Design 4 implications

The locked Design 4 brief remains reasonable for discovery:

- two detached 22×22 garages;
- far-rear placement using 5 ft rear and 5 ft side targets;
- Pennsylvania-only access;
- no living/sleeping space;
- at least 6 ft from each principal dwelling;
- rear-yard garage height to remain within the applicable R-12 accessory limit unless a later verified scenario changes the placement/rule.

## Important unresolved spacing issue

The true Lot 2 polygon is only about 56.2 ft wide at x≈5. Two 22 ft garage plates consume 44 ft. Holding approximately 5 ft at both outer side boundaries leaves only about **2.2 ft between the two garage plates** if both sit on the same rear line.

The zoning sections above establish the property-line setbacks and garage-to-principal-structure relationship, but this review does **not** establish that a ~2 ft garage-to-garage separation is acceptable under the applicable building/fire code, eave/projection rules, drainage/snow requirements, or parcel-specific interpretation.

Therefore the rear garage pair may be used as a **discovery geometry**, but its inter-garage spacing is a **professional/AHJ confirmation item** before the pair is treated as a buildable lock. If a larger separation is required, Design 4 should stagger one garage forward while preserving the full 22×22 plates rather than shrinking them.
