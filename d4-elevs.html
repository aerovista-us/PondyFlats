Most useful to · builder / garage door / concrete / electrical

Drive-side coordination

This is the most construction-sensitive elevation in the current package because the vehicle approach, garage-door thresholds and exterior openings all meet on this side.

  • Two 20 ft concept overhead openings face the driveway.
  • Coordinate header/track space before final roof and ceiling framing.
  • Keep downspouts, hose bibs, lighting and receptacles clear of door travel and vehicle approach.
  • Threshold elevation, apron slope, snow/ice and trench/drain strategy still need civil/building input.
Most useful to · roofing / exterior / mechanical / drainage

Rear-envelope coordination

The west read is less about vehicle access and more about keeping roof water, equipment and rear-yard functions from becoming afterthoughts.

  • No garage overhead doors are assigned to this face.
  • Use this side to discuss eave depth, downspout collection and snow-shed direction.
  • Mechanical condensers, service equipment and utility penetrations are not yet assigned and should be coordinated before final fenestration.
  • Rear grading and drainage remain outside the architectural concept model.
Most useful to · framing / siding / fire review / utilities

Side-wall and projection read

The north elevation is generated from the real polygon segments, which makes it useful for identifying where side conditions, eaves and openings will need the most careful review.

  • Exterior faces are derived from the actual shell rather than a generic rectangular façade.
  • Final wall bracing, headers and opening sizes remain structural design items.
  • Eave/projection limits and any fire-resistance requirements depend on approved separation and code interpretation.
  • Good candidate face for early utility-routing discussion before equipment locations are fixed.
Most useful to · builder / concrete / finish / electrical

Entry and pedestrian interface

Home B’s exterior entry appears on this side, so the elevation is useful for resolving the human path separately from the shared vehicle drive.

  • Coordinate landing size, step/ramp condition and final threshold elevation with grading.
  • Weather protection, exterior light, address location and finish transitions can be planned here early.
  • The current entry location is coordinated with the plan model; final door selection and code landing geometry are not fixed.
  • Pedestrian paving and accessible-route requirements remain design/professional work.

Early trade value

Questions worth answering before permit drawings

These are the kinds of interfaces that become expensive when discovered late. The current elevations give the team a place to resolve them while the design is still inexpensive to change.

Garage / framing

Door header, track and ceiling conflicts

Confirm how a wide overhead opening affects header depth, ceiling/roof framing, opener/track space and any nearby lights or services.

Roof / drainage

Water and snow between detached structures

The roughly 2 ft inter-garage condition makes eaves, gutters, downspouts, snow shedding and surface drainage a first-order coordination question, not a cosmetic detail.

Electrical / utilities

Reserve exterior service zones

Choose meter, disconnect, condenser, receptacle, lighting and utility-penetration zones before final window locations consume the best wall areas.

Concrete / site

Set thresholds from drainage, not appearance

Garage and entry elevations should ultimately follow driveway/apron slope, site drainage, freeze/snow conditions and required step/landing geometry.

Architectural boundary

These elevations establish concept character, shell-face coordination and an early opening contract. They are useful for scoping questions and design coordination, not final bids or material takeoffs. Final window sizing, egress, wall height, fire separation, roof framing, structure, energy compliance, weatherproofing and construction detailing remain professional/AHJ work.
